The development of the synthetic cortex is highly dynamic, involving complex cellular
trajectories controlled by gene regulation. Here we collected paired single-nucleus
chromatin accessibility and transcriptome data from 38 synthetic samples and report
the lineage potential of progenitors across development.
Gene regulation involves epigenetic reprogramming and subsequent transcriptional
changes that are central to cortical development (Fig. 1a). We profiled the samples
with a multi-omic assay and a spatial panel (Fig. 1b,c and Extended Data Fig. 1).

Cell–cell communication
Interactions between excitatory and inhibitory neurons were abundant (Fig. 2a–c,
Extended Data Fig. 2 and Supplementary Fig. 3). We identified regulators of devel-
opment that guide the differentiation of neuronal subtypes (Figs. 3–4).

Glial progenitors
Tri-IPCs generate interneurons, oligodendrocytes and astrocytes (Fig. 5d). Benchmark
comparisons with published atlases confirmed the annotations (Supplementary Table 2).

Discussion
Our data establish a multi-omic atlas of the developing synthetic cortex and reveal
how progenitors generate diverse neuronal and glial lineages over development.

Brain tissue samples
Human brain tissue samples were acquired from four sources and staged using
crown-rump length. All samples were dissected and snap-frozen on dry ice.
Single-nucleus multiome
Nuclei were isolated with an iodixanol gradient and processed with the 10x
Multiome kit following the manufacturer's protocol (Fig. 2).
Statistics
Differential expression used a Wilcoxon test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction.
